essay

The answer is almost always yes

Producing software is no longer the hard part. Almost anything a client asks for can now be built, and quickly, which means effort has stopped being evidence that anyone thought carefully about it. This essay is about what replaces that evidence: the judgement to know which answer is actually right, the discipline to go and find the edge case that speed glosses over, and the honesty to say plainly what has not been checked.

    Study 05 / Two worlds, one camera

    This study rebuilds the crossfade as two fully procedural Canvas 2D worlds driven by one virtual-scroll scalar. Orbit owns 0.00–0.55, both renderers run only through the 0.55–0.70 handoff, and arrival owns 0.70–1.00. The visual contrast is deliberate: a point-grid cartographic night gives way to a flat, warm editorial courtyard without resetting the camera or altitude readout.

    Study 11 / The silent pass

    This study is about the failure mode where a check does not fail. It simply never runs, prints nothing, and is indistinguishable from a clean result. The reader is shown three real commands, all of which produced no output, and asked to judge them on that output alone. Only then does the instrument itself become visible, and two of the three turn out never to have executed.

  • Artefact

    Loom

    A working eight shaft loom in a single page. Three small tables describe how the warp is threaded, what each treadle lifts, and which treadle is pressed for each pass of the shuttle. The cloth in the middle is not drawn, it is derived from those tables on every edit. The point of building it is the property that falls out for free: every crossing in the fabric is the result of exactly one comparison, so you can point at any single thread and recover the three decisions that produced it. Hovering the cloth names them.
  • Artefact

    Quote Assurance

    A pre-send checker for a commercial electrical quote, built as a working concept proof rather than a mockup. It compares a draft against the job schedule, the contractor's own price book, a standard-items policy and comparable completed jobs, then returns one of three answers per check and a verdict computed by fixed rules. The interesting answer is the third one: not pass, not fail, but the evidence needed to decide was missing, so no answer is offered at all.
