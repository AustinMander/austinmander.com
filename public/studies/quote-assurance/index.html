Every check here returns one of three answers, and the third one is the point. PASS means it was checked and it held. FAIL means it was checked and it did not. CANNOT VERIFY means the evidence needed to check it was missing, so no answer is offered at all. That last state is drawn unfilled and dashed rather than in a warning colour, because it is not a milder kind of failure. It is the absence of a check, and the whole argument is that those two things must never look alike.

So the checker cannot pass by being agreeable. Two of the ten drafts are clean controls that must come back SEND, which is what stops it flagging everything and calling that diligence. One draft has no schedule to compare against and one line with no price source, and the correct behaviour there is to produce no scope verdict, no margin and no exposure figure. It says what it could not check and what it would need. It does not reach for a market average to fill the hole.

The verdict is arithmetic, not prose. Any missing evidence or any high finding is a HOLD; anything medium is a REVIEW; otherwise SEND. No summary, confidence score or interface action can promote it. And SEND means only that these checks passed on these source versions. It is not approval, and it does not send anything.

Quote Assurance, concept proof. The seven checks and the verdict policy are implemented from a written specification and live in engine.js, apart from this page, so they can be run and scored without a browser. The fixture matrix is frozen: ten drafts, eight with a declared seeded fault, two clean controls. The acceptance run scores catch rate and clean-send rate separately, because a checker that flags everything scores full marks on catches alone.
